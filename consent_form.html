General Public Volunteer Study: Consent Form

We are asking you to participate in a research study titled “Crowdsourcing Transparency for Chatbot Personalization”. We will describe this study to you and answer any of your questions. This study is being led by Angelina Wang, Information Science Department at Cornell University, along with Eve Fleisig, Information Science Department at Cornell University. This study is being funded by a grant from the Toyota Research Institute.

What the study is about

When different people use chatbots, they often get different responses to the same question. However, the scope, causes, and effects of this personalization remain opaque. The goal of this research is to create a crowd-sourced transparency initiative using a browser extension to collect standardized AI responses across diverse user populations. This will allow users to share their responses and compare them with others, promoting transparency of how responses are personalized; and allow researchers to study whether responses are personalized in harmful ways (e.g. more incorrect, toxic, or sycophantic responses given to some users).

What we will ask you to do

We will ask you to install a browser extension that will periodically prompt you to submit a series of questions to ChatGPT, then submit the response on the browser extension. We will ask you to rate the quality of the response, which helps us to study whether some user groups receive better responses than other. You may submit as many or as few responses as you like. You may also submit responses to new questions that you ask ChatGPT. You will also be asked to optionally provide information about your demographics and your usage of AI-based software. These questions help us to identify whether chatbots discriminate against some users. You may choose not to respond to any of the demographic questions with no penalty to you.

Risks and discomforts

ChatGPT responses may include personal information based on your past interaction with ChatGPT. We include a redaction tool so that you can remove any personal information you do not wish to share before submitting the response to us.

Benefits

There are no direct benefits to participants. By participating in the study, you can see how the responses that you receive from ChatGPT differ from the responses that other people receive.

Information from this study aims to benefit the study of whether there is inequality in the responses that people get from AI chatbots, so that we can improve the quality of responses and help to warn of potential risks of harmful personalization.

Incentives for participation

There is no monetary compensation for the general public. By participating in this study, you will be able to see how your responses from ChatGPT compare to those received by other users.

Privacy/Confidentiality/Data Security

Identifying information that you provide to us (your email) will be kept separate from the extension data.

Please note that ChatGPT responses may sometimes include identifying information about the user. To protect your privacy, we will remove identifying information in responses before releasing de-identified versions of this data, which will be made available to the research community in order to further study the effects of chatbot personalization. Identifying information that is not redacted may be accessed by members of the research team.

We will take the following steps to protect participant privacy and confidentiality:

  • Participants may redact any part of the responses
  • Participants may choose not to submit the response to any question we ask

All data will be stored on Cornell University clusters, on Supabase, and on password-protected computers. Requests are transferred via ngrok, and logins use Google Firebase. The text of your response, after you submit and redact it, is sent to ChatGPT in order to calculate the similarities between your response and other responses.

We will do our best to keep your participation in this research study confidential to the extent permitted by law; however, it is possible that other people may need to review the research records and may find out about your participation in this study. For example, the following people/groups may check and copy records about this research:

  • The Office for Human Research Protections in the U. S. Department of Health and Human Services
  • The research study sponsor, Toyota Research Institute
  • Cornell University’s Institutional Review Board (a committee that reviews and approves research studies) and the Office for Research Integrity and Assurance

We anticipate that your participation in this study presents no greater risk than everyday use of the Internet.

Data may exist on backups and server logs beyond the timeframe of this research project.

Sharing De-identified Data Collected in this Research

De-identified data from this study may be shared with the research community at large to advance science. We will remove or code any personal information that could identify you before files are shared with other researchers to ensure that, by current scientific standards and known methods, no one will be able to identify you from the information we share. Despite these measures, we cannot guarantee anonymity of your personal data.

Future use of Identifiable Data Collected in this Research

Identifiers might be removed and the de-identified information used for future research without additional consent.

Taking part is voluntary

Your participation in this study is voluntary. You may refuse to participate before the study begins, discontinue at any time, or skip any questions/procedures that may make you feel uncomfortable, with no penalty to you. You may choose not to participate if you are uncomfortable with these conditions.

Follow up studies

We may contact you again to request your participation in a follow up study. As always, your participation will be voluntary and we will ask for your explicit consent to participate in any of the follow up studies.
